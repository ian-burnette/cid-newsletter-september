Abstract

A college degree remains as perhaps the final remaining institutionalized pathway to middle class socioeconomic attainment, with recent college graduates attaining historically unprecedented earnings premiums. In this project I recover a novel and unappreciated trend that coexists with this overall rising college premium: the rate of college graduates experiencing unambiguously negative labor market outcomes has been growing for 25 years. Applying a relative distributional methodological approach to seven datasets spanning 75 years, this project reveals the processes leading to rising economic risk among college graduates.

About the speaker

Tom VanHeuvelen is an associate professor of sociology at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities and is affiliated with the Minnesota Population Center and Life Course Center. His research interests include inequality and stratification, quantitative methodology, political sociology, the sociology of development, and historical and comparative sociology.
