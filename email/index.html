SWIPE’s Whose Democracy? initiative begins with Who Runs?

 
 

Who has the opportunity to run for political office, and what do those opportunities mean for democratic representation?

 

This September, the Stone Working Group on Inequality and Political Economy, or SWIPE, launches Whose Democracy?, a two-year initiative examining how inequality shapes democracy.

Its opening event, Who Runs?, will feature Noam Lupu of Vanderbilt University and Nick Carnes of Duke University. The conversation will consider how class and resources influence who runs for office, who is treated as a viable candidate, and whose interests are ultimately represented.

The event is free and open to the U-M community. It is presented with support from the Initiative for Democracy and Civic Empowerment as part of Democracy Week 2026.

New Faculty Associate
 

Welcome, Thomas Crossley

 

We are pleased to welcome Thomas Crossley to the Stone Center faculty community as a faculty associate.

Crossley is Research Professor in the Survey Research Center and director of the Panel Study of Income Dynamics (PSID). His research examines household behavior, financial security and living standards, economic measurement, and the design and analysis of survey data.

We look forward to the perspectives and expertise he will bring to the CID community.

 
Research and Recognition
 
Food Insecurity Research
 

Noura Insolera’s research connects food insecurity and dementia risk

 

Stone Center faculty associate Noura Insolera and collaborators recently published new research examining food insecurity and dementia risk among U.S. adults. The study, published in The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, received coverage from Michigan News and Newsweek.

Insolera was also featured by U-M Research in a story about the federal government’s decision to end its long-running annual food insecurity report and the importance of continued measurement.

Insolera and Stone Center faculty associate Thomas Crossley are also co-principal investigators on Monitoring Food Hardship in the U.S., a new U-M Strategic Initiative Fund project led by Stone Center affiliate Natasha Pilkauskas.

New Research
 

Robert Manduca maps the shifting geography of racial inequality

 

Robert Manduca published “Shift, Not Stasis: The Geography of Post–Civil Rights Racial Inequality” in the American Journal of Sociology. The article shows that persistent national Black-white income disparities have been accompanied by substantial changes in their geography since 1969.

2026 Significant Scholarship Award
 

Pablo Mitnik receives 2026 Significant Scholarship Award

 

Congratulations to Pablo Mitnik, Victoria Bryant, and David Grusky, whose article, “A Very Uneven Playing Field: Economic Mobility in the United States,” received the 2026 Significant Scholarship Award from the International Sociological Association’s Research Committee on Social Stratification and Mobility.

Published in the American Journal of Sociology, the article examines economic mobility and the persistence of economic advantage in the United States. It also received an honorable mention for the American Sociological Association Section on Inequality, Poverty, and Mobility’s 2025 Devah Pager Outstanding Article Award.

Emerging Inequality Scholars
 

New research from former Emerging Inequality Scholar So’Phelia Morrow

 

Former Emerging Inequality Scholar So’Phelia Morrow has published “Not the Typical Victim: Exploring the Costs of Intimate Partner Violence on Middle-Class Black Women” in Race and Social Problems.

Drawing on interviews with 15 participants, the study examines the financial consequences of intimate partner violence for middle-class Black women. It identifies themes involving control over money, debt burdens, uneven access to formal resources, and reduced opportunities to save or build wealth.

The research received support from the Stone Center through the Emerging Inequality Scholars program.

American Sociological Association · 2026
 

ASA recognition across the Stone Center community

We are pleased to celebrate Stone Center scholars, associates, affiliates, and alumni whose work received recognition from the American Sociological Association this year.

 
 

Erin Ice, a former Stone Center graduate student affiliate, received the ASA Dissertation Award for My Mother’s Keeper: The Singularity of Caregiving in the U.S.

 
 

Jeremy R. Levine, Stone Center associate and associate director, received the Crime, Law, and Deviance Section’s James F. Short Jr. Distinguished Article Award for “Whose Victimization Pays? Policing Innocent Victimhood in Victim Compensation Law,” published in Law & Society Review.

 
 

Greta R. Krippner, Stone Center faculty affiliate, received two honors for “Gendered Market Devices: The Persistence of Gender Discrimination in Insurance Markets,” published in the American Journal of Sociology: the Economic Sociology Section’s Granovetter Award for Best Article in Economic Sociology and the Sociology of Sex and Gender Section’s Distinguished Article Award.

 
 

Christopher I. Rider, Stone Center faculty affiliate, received the Organizations, Occupations, and Work Section’s Richard Scott Article Award with James B. Wade, Anand Swaminathan, and Andreas Schwab for “Racial Disparity in Leadership: Evidence of Valuative Bias in the Promotions of National Football League Coaches.”

 
 

Jun Zhou, Stone Center graduate student affiliate, received an honorable mention for the Sociology of Sex and Gender Section’s Hacker-Mullins Student Paper Award for “Doing Data, Doing Gender: Manufacturing Gendered AI through the Optimization Loop,” published in Big Data & Society.

 
 

Robert Manduca, Stone Center faculty associate, received honorable mentions for two articles: “Tax Base Fragmentation as a Dimension of Metropolitan Inequality,” with Brian Highsmith and Jacob Waggoner, for the Community and Urban Sociology Section’s Jane Addams Article Award; and “Should Social Insurance Programs Count as Wealth? Augmented Wealth in Research and Policy” for the Economic Sociology Section’s Granovetter Award for Best Article.

New research from the Stone Center community
 
 

Robert Manduca and former Stone Center student associate Jane Furey published “Nationally Ahead, Locally Behind: Geographic Scale and the Asian American Income (Dis)advantage” in the Russell Sage Foundation Journal of the Social Sciences. The article examines how the apparent socioeconomic advantage of Asian Americans changes depending on whether inequality is measured nationally or within local labor markets.

 
 

Kimberly Rollings and Hannah Lang published “Rural–urban comparisons of area-based socioeconomic disadvantage and vulnerability indices” in Health Affairs Scholar. Their study finds that commonly used measures can classify community needs differently and introduce systematic rural-urban biases into decisions about resources.
